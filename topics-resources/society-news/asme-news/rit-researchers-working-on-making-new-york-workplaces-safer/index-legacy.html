    RIT Researchers Working on Making New York Workplaces Safer

    Share on LinkedIn Share on Facebook Share via email
    RIT researchers working with grocery store employees to improve workplace ergonomics and reduce injuries.

    The Rochester Institute of Technology has received a Susan Harwood Training Grant from the U.S. Department of Labor to expand its Occupational Safety and Ergonomics Excellence Program, delivering free, on-site ergonomic consultations and training to small and medium-sized grocery stores across New York State.

    Led by lead Graduate Researcher Evans Debrah under the supervision of Dr. Eugene Park Wiezer, Ph.D., Assistant Director of Technical Programs, the project will provide walk-through risk assessments, hands-on training, and confidential recommendations to reduce musculoskeletal disorders caused by repetitive motion and material handling. The initiative is expected to benefit at least 150 businesses with fewer than 200 employees, helping them cut workers' compensation costs, reduce lost work hours, and boost productivity.

    "This grant allows our team to meet businesses where they are—on the shop floor—so we can translate ergonomics science into practical fixes that prevent injuries," said Dr. Eugene, principal investigator and associate professor at RIT. "Evans brings exceptional rigor and empathy to this work; his field leadership ensures that our recommendations are realistic, cost-effective, and immediately actionable for grocery operators statewide."

    Evans Debrah added, "We're focused on simple changes that make a big difference—from workstation layout and lift-assist tools to smarter scheduling and task rotation. When workers feel better, businesses perform better."

    An Assistant Secretary for Occupational Safety and Health at the U.S. Department of Labor praised the collaboration: "The Susan Harwood Training Grants connect expertise to the workers and employers who need it most. RIT's program exemplifies this mission and will strengthen protections for employees in high-hazard retail environments while improving the bottom line for 150 New York businesses."
